A portfolio for Yashvardhan Sonegara, built around the gearbox he 3D-printed for physics. Visitors shift through his work like gears.

THE IDEA

The website is the gearbox.

Five shifter positions open five chapters. Neutral introduces him. Each gear opens one project, in the order of the real gearbox, so the Mercedes hackathon project sits in overdrive. The tooth counts on screen are the real ones, and each output gear spins at the RPM he measured.

THE DATA

Why it's real

Ratio is output teeth over drive teeth. Output RPM is rotations counted over one minute in each gear. The drive gear spins at output × ratio, so the teeth stay in mesh on screen.

PHASE 2 HANDOFF

Notes for the build

Everything phase 2 needs to make the screens behave the way they do on this canvas.

Shifter knobsReal buttons. aria-pressed marks the current gear. Idle: dark fill, grey ring. Hover: orange ring. Engaged: orange fill with an 8 px glow. Keyboard focus: 3 px orange outline.
Spinning gearsOutput gear turns at the measured RPM. Drive gear turns at output × ratio so the teeth stay in mesh. Both restart together on every shift.
NeutralThe drive gear keeps turning, like a running motor. The output gear slides away, dims and stops. RPM reads 0.
Rev needle0 to 120 rpm across 180°. Sweeps in 0.7 s with a slight overshoot when you change gear.
Reduced motionIf the visitor has turned on reduced motion, the gears hold still. The numbers and needle still update.
ResponsiveOne fluid page. Gears and shifter sit side by side from iPad width up and stack on phones. On phones the menu collapses to Contact and the logbook drops its Field column.
One page, three screens, a case study. To the right of this board: desktop at 1440 px, iPad at 820 px and phone at 390 px. They are the same responsive page, each opened in a different gear. Press Play on any of them and use the shifter.
